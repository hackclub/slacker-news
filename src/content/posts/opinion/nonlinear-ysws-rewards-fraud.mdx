Hack Club has a truly interesting structure for You Ship, We Ship projects. We have so many YSWS of all shapes and sizes, all with their quirks, benefits, and themes. 

This format is extremely unique, but allows us to do some really cool things! It allows us to test ideas, improve upon each other, and most of all, employ different techniques to achieve the same goal: ship cool stuff to teenagers making cool projects.

Nearly every YSWS does it differently: #macando used tiers, #stardance uses peer-reviewing, #pixl uses a ladder that allows you to get better rates as you ship more, #wonders (upcoming) completely ignores hours and payouts and just ships you something cool for the work you do. And I think we can take advantage of this structure to solve (some) of our fraud problems.

# The problem
I’m going into this assuming that the only reason for fraud in other countries (namely India) is because the dollar is weighted so heavily there. At least, that's the problem I’m attempting to combat.

On one hand, it's unfair on participants from these other countries that are honestly putting the work in, if we lower regional rates. Because, as Zach mentioned, electronics and flights cost the same no matter where you get them from. In fact, after doing a bit of research on about 10 items in the Stardance shop, most cost about the same if not more to purchase in India. 

On the other hand, it's true that where you are plays a factor. Buying a 3D printer in India is objectively harder (from a monetary perspective, not fulfillment) than buying a 3D printer in the US. Therefore, the argument can be made that Hack Club should reflect that to some extent.

# My solution
My solution isn’t trying to solve that problem directly. My solution is to answer this question: “How can we reduce fraud in countries where Hack Club payouts are worth so much, while limiting their disadvantages?” 

This is where I come back to the point about YSWSs. I suggest that *flagship YSWSs use a non-linear rate.* As you ship more, your rewards increase more. This system would apply to all countries. It can be accomplished in 2 main ways:
- A 5-hour project shipped will always receive worse rates than a 10-hour project.
  - Most fraud projects that pass through Fraud Squad (this is an assumption) are smaller projects. This minimizes losses on fraud projects. In addition, it encourages people to make larger projects
  - People that make amazing projects but just work quickly can ship to YSWSs like #wonders.
- A Hack Club/YSWS wide trust ladder system.
  - The more hours you have shipped to Hack Club, the less likely you are to do fraud. Once you have shipped projects, they add to a counter of your total approved hours. As your hours pass certain milestones, you unlock better and better funding.
  - This can create “tiers” of people (“Oh I’m a level 4 shipper, you are only level 2”) creating a hierarchy that has no place in Hack Club
    - Can be combatted by just keeping this private info instead of making it public
  - Encourages people to keep shipping to Hack Club, however, it might overwhelm beginners that feel like they will never be able to reach higher levels of funding
    - Can be combatted by giving beginners the ability to ship to YSWSs that have higher funding for shipping/attending huddles that follow guides (because these are extremely unlikely to be fraud).

One downside to this whole system is that it might encourage even more inflation of hours. However, I think this is already incentivized a lot, and I trust in Fraud Squad to be able to detect this pretty easily.

I understand beginners might be disappointed by this model. That’s why I suggest having more beginner-friendly YSWSs and opportunities for us to “waive” the reduced rates and give a better rate instead, maybe by holding huddles where if you ship a project by the end, you get a better rate, or having extremely specific guides they can follow.

I’m also not very sure how hardware works… (I’ve never shipped hardware) so I’ll leave that up to you.
