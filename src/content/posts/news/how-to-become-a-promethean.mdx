I’m getting more and more requests to use Prometheus. This is great! Demand is the mark of a good product.

Managing all these requests is getting to be a lot. But rather than just posting a public policy and leaving it at that, let me explain why that’s the policy, and how it could change.

# Who can get them now?
When someone asks me for Prometheus powers, I look for three things:
1. You’re asking about a channel that is a public utility.
2. You haven’t done anything shady or immature.
3. You have a vision for making the channel great.

(I also give them out at the request of HQ employees to manage channels for their programs)

# Requests I turn down:
“This channel could use more moderation”. Often true! But, we have one moderation team, the Fire Department. This has downsides (they are very busy) but it keeps moderation accountable to HQ and the community, and ensures moderation can be changed as needed.
“Can I get it for my personal channel?” No, for philosophical reasons I’ll get to later.
“I think this channel should be threaded.” This is often a matter of personal taste, rather than of making a channel great.

I want the Slack to have great channels where people love spending time. Prometheus a tool. My vision is bigger than the tool and I want yours to be too. I want to see that you have a plan for an awesome channel, and then you can have Prometheus to make it happen.

# Personal Channels
I’ve written before about how I’m nervous about private channels. I have no interest in putting Prometheus in private channels, because I’m not convinced they are a net good for the community.

*Public* personal channels, I’m less sure about. The current problem is oversight: the powers could be abused, and I need to oversee the channels personally. There are far too many personal channels to apply it to all of them, and I don’t want to take on the job of picking and choosing which channels get it. That will create ugly conflicts, and leave me too little time for other projects.

# We’re not chained to a rock
When I started as community manager, the Slack had 1300 daily active users and 5000 monthly active users. Now it has 2400 daily actives and 8000 monthly actives. “Trends will continue” is always a solid bet in the short term, so by the end of the year, I expect our community will roughly double in size. 

What makes sense in a community of 1500 won’t always make sense in a community of 3000, or 6000. We can’t stop the Slack from changing, but we can shape the change. I’m thinking a lot about what the Slack should look like as it grows.

Could expanding the use of Prometheus be one way we deal with our inevitable growing pains? Maybe. I see two problems:
1. Scalable oversight - Someone needs to ensure Prometheans don’t misuse their powers. Right now that’s me. But there’s only one of me. If we’re going to have 10x more Prometheans, we need a reliable, fast, and consistent system to prevent abuse of power.
2. Fragmentation - In my opinion, this is already the community’s most serious problem. It’s just so big and diffuse that *nobody* has a complete picture of what is going on, and it’s very hard to make community-wide changes. Having many separate channels each empowered to go their own way will likely make this work.

I would be open to using Prometheus more widely if I saw good solutions to these problems. This piece is my invitation to you, to think about them and share your ideas.
