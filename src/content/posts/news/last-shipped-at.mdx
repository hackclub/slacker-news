The desert of theory goes on forever, and you can wander it forever imagining things that wouldn't really work, believing they would. Only by doing will you finally know. And actually shipping a project teaches another lesson that theorizing can't: you (yes, you!) can change things. If you don't like your choices, you can color outside the lines and make new choices, even if an adult never gave you permission.

That's why we're here. We want you to make friends. But we want you to make friends by bonding over shipping projects. We want you to learn. But we want you to learn by shipping projects. We want to change your life. But we want to change your life by shipping projects.

So, did you ship yet? If not, why not? If yes, how long ago? Are you working on shipping something else? If not, why not? What are you doing to keep growing? What if you’re wandering the desert of theory?

Your Slack profile now has an API-controlled field "Last shipped a project". Special thanks to <SlackMention name="nora" id="U09ULFV88KU" /> for pushing aside the soldering irons and ethernet switches on her desk long enough to make this happen. The Slack API documentation is...uhh...well, I would never criticize it for having too much information.

This is a device to help you hold yourself accountable. Do you feel subtly pressured to ship a project now? That's the idea! You don't have to, but...maybe you want to? To get that number down? Good, good. If you're new, this encourages you to break the Ship Barrier, because there's nothing like your first ship. It will probably be junk, but it will also change you by showing that you (yes, you!) can actually be a hacker instead of just wishing you were one. If you are not new, this is to stop you getting complacent. Once you do the thing, it's easy to say "I am a person who has done the thing." But we always have more to learn.

This is also a device to keep the community focused where it should be. When a community discusses or debates, normally the most outspoken, the most persistent, or the most eloquent voices get the most influence. But none of these correlates with who is most correct. Shipping projects shouldn’t be the only thing that gets people listening to you, but it should be one of them, and now it is.

Getting a project into the DB updates your field automatically. Since things take a while to hit the DB, the field won’t be perfectly accurate. But stuff need not be perfect to be good. Manual overrides are possible, but we are not promising them. There are perhaps 5 people in all of Hack Club whom I would give a manual override for their work with me. If you are assuming you'll get a manual override, let that assumption go. Ship to a YSWS! Is the YSWS model not working for you? Do you have great projects, but our current programs can't accommodate you? Tell us! We want to hear about that. 40 gap years will fight over you.

What are you shipping next?
