Hack Clubbers suggest YSWS ideas every day, but most end up missing the bar for sponsorship. This means the idea can't be successful, right? Wrong. The answer lies in the pitch.

Over the last few months working at HQ I've noticed that we have practically unlimited, amazing ideas for programs, but loads of them never make it into existence. They disappear into the void forever because it seems like no one wants to give them a shot!

The truth is that a good pitch can massively increase your chances of sponsorship. Since your pitch gives HQ the first glimpse at how your program will run and at its chances of success, it's important to get it right the first time. So, here's how to pitch a YSWS for the highest chance of getting sponsored: the right way.

## Start with a draft

So, you have an awesome YSWS idea, but you need a sponsor[^sponsor]! What do you do? (And what _don't_ you do?)

First, I recommend making a good YSWS draft. That could be a one-page Google Doc (recommended!), alongside a website, artwork, or anything else that proves you can execute your idea. Gap years[^gappies] take on a lot of risk when they sponsor something, so we're only likely to sponsor things we think will succeed!

## Tips for your one-pager

**Open with your hook.** That's a one-sentence description of your YSWS. For example:

- "Make a game, build an arcade in Japan." (Shiba)
- "Build for 50 hours, fly to an escape room hackathon in Philadelphia." (Phantom)
- "Learn Pygame, get prizesss." (Snek)

**Say whether your YSWS is general or themed.**

If it's themed, make sure your participants still make technical projects[^technical]: coding or electronics! Non-technical projects can't be shipped to Unified[^unified], so non-technical YSWSs are unlikely to get sponsored. No ships in Unified means no weighted projects[^wp], and no weighted projects means no funding.

**Include an example project!**

The best way to prove you can support your participants is to do your own program. Incoming gap years are told: "You are the CEO of your own program, so you are responsible for all areas of your program." That applies to your events too! Your sponsor will help you, but you need to put yourself in your participants' shoes and get a feel for your own program. An example project also helps gappies understand what your program is about.

**Know your target audience.**

- Complete beginners: how are you supporting their learning? Think workshops and guides.
- Advanced hackers: how are you pushing them to further their knowledge?
- Or somewhere in between?

**Have a budget.**

Do a little research into the weighted projects system. It's how your program will fund itself, so you should really have an idea of how it works. Try to budget around $5 per hour of coding, in line with what gappies are recommended. If you're pitching an in-person event, think about how you'll fund that too. Venues, transport, food and lodging all cost money. Where is it coming from?

**Be realistic.** Can you execute this with your current skill set? If not, do you have a realistic plan for how you'll make it happen? Show us!

**Be open to changing and iterating on your idea!**

A gap year asking for changes is not a no. It means we'd be open to sponsoring your program with a few tweaks. Changes are usually requested for a reason, often an incompatibility with the Unified DB, the budget, or participant safety.

## Don't spam HQ

Second: don't just spam everyone at HQ with your pitch. That's a sure-fire way to _not_ get a sponsor, and to annoy literally everyone in the office. We talk to each other about these things. Once a pitch has reached a few of us, it's reached most of us.

## Finding the right gap year

**Do your research!** Like everyone else, we gappies have our own interests, and those shape what we're willing to sponsor. Sending a software-focused gappie a pitch for a hardware hackathon probably won't get you sponsored, and it wastes both your time and theirs. Target your pitches for a higher success rate <Emoji name="yay" />. You can get an idea of someone's niche by checking out the programs they've run before <Emoji name="agadance" />.

**Please don't DM every single gap year.** There's a reason most of the gappie lists floating around Slack say not to do this. We talk to each other, and spamming basically guarantees you don't get sponsored <Emoji name="heavysob" />. When we decide what to sponsor, we're also deciding who we want to work with! A good first impression makes your pitch way more likely to get sponsored.

A surprising number of people forget that we all work in the same office five days a week.

So what are you waiting for? Go make that YSWS idea real!

[^sponsor]: **Sponsor**: the gap year who provides the funding and Hack Club infrastructure to run your program, in exchange for the weighted projects it generates.

[^gappies]: **Gap years / gappies**: in-person staff at Hack Club HQ in Vermont, working full-time for a year on the YSWS team.

[^technical]: **Technical project**: a project that can be "shipped" to the Unified DB and receive funding based on the time spent making it.

[^unified]: **Unified / Unified DB / the DB**: the big Airtable database where every project shipped to Hack Club ends up.

[^wp]: **Weighted projects (WPs)**: the metric gap years are measured on, also used to allocate funding to programs. 1 WP = 10 hours of coding = $85, with $50 of that used for prize fulfillment.
